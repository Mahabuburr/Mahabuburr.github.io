Projects

From research models to working systems.

Flagship project

Predictive maintenance platform for railway fleet assets

Maintenance teams often hold years of fault and maintenance records across separate systems, but have no simple way to see which assets are likely to fail next or where to act first. This project turns those records into forecasts, explanations and ranked maintenance priorities that engineers can use in their daily work. It extends my doctoral research with Siemens Mobility from individual models into a working end-to-end prototype.

  • Data layer: multi-year fault, maintenance and service-impact records are cleaned, validated and stored in Microsoft SQL Server, so every forecast and explanation is traceable.
  • Forecasting: Bidirectional GRU models predict future failures at fleet and unit level, with rolling retraining when a new operating period is completed and model-version checks to avoid outdated models.
  • Explainable AI: post-hoc temporal perturbation analysis shows which past periods drive each forecast, so engineers can see why a prediction changed.
  • Maintenance prioritisation: multi-criteria decision analysis ranks maintenance candidates using risk, failure history, operational impact, cost and safety criteria, tested with Monte Carlo simulation.
  • Services and dashboards: FastAPI REST endpoints serve predictions to Power BI dashboards showing forecasts, risk levels, explanations and priorities.
  • Agentic AI assistant: a locally hosted LLM (Ollama, Qwen2.5:7b) answers engineers' questions using verified data from SQL, with approved tools only and human approval before protected steps such as retraining.

Outcome: a repeatable workflow that moves from raw maintenance records to explained, prioritised maintenance decisions, supporting a shift from reactive to planned, risk-informed maintenance.
